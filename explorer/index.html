How mark-to-market works

Positions are carried at fair value, not cost. Each night the change in fair value becomes the day's P&L, the basis for margin calls, and the number risk and finance report on.

What gets marked

Each asset class has its own price convention, and the engine needs to know which one it is reading.

ClassPrice used
EquitiesExchange close (last)
BondsClean price % of par + accrued
FuturesExchange settlement price
FX forwardsForward outright, discounted

Fair value hierarchy

IFRS 13 and ASC 820 grade every mark by how observable its input is. Lower levels get more scrutiny.

L1Quoted price for the identical instrument in an active market. Listed equities, futures, on-the-run government bonds.
L2Observable inputs other than a direct quote. Evaluated bond prices, FX forwards built from spot and points.
L3Unobservable inputs and model assumptions. Private credit, bespoke derivatives.

The nightly cycle (SGT)

  1. 17:00 TAsian markets close; exchange settlement prices published.
  2. 18:30 TPrimary and secondary vendor files land; FX snapshot taken.
  3. 19:00 TValidate, normalise, value. STP controls run.
  4. 21:00 TPrice challenge cut-off for exceptions.
  5. 07:00 T+1Valuation, P&L and IPV reports to finance, risk and the fund administrator.
  6. 09:00 T+1Margin call notices issued under each CSA.

The pipeline

Seven steps from raw vendor files to distributed reports. Counts update live as you change market data, rules and resolutions below.

    1

    Inputs: positions and market data

    Positions come from the books of record at end of day. Prices come from two independent vendors so they can be checked against each other. Edit any price, clear a box to simulate a missing feed, or load a scenario.

    Instrument prices

    FX rates (units per 1 USD)

    Reading the inputs

    The prior mark is yesterday's signed-off official price; it is the anchor for daily P&L and the day-on-day check.

    The primary feed is the golden source. The secondary exists for independent price verification and as a fallback.

    A primary price that equals yesterday's mark and carries an old date is the classic sign of a stale evaluated price.

    2

    Transformation: from vendor record to valuation

    Vendors send prices in their own layouts. The engine normalises each record into one internal schema, enriches it with static data, FX and the prior mark, then applies the asset-class formula. Pick an instrument to trace its lineage.

    3

    STP business rules

    Straight-through processing means a mark is released with no human touch. Every price passes six controls; anything that fails stops in the exception queue. Adjust the thresholds and watch the STP rate move.

    Control results

    PASS = control passed · FIX = auto-remediated within policy (still STP) · FAIL = exception · 4-EYE = needs a second approver · OFF = rule disabled

    4

    Exception queue

    Failed controls wait here for a valuation analyst. Each resolution records the price chosen and the reason, which feeds the audit trail in the IPV report. Reports stay in DRAFT until the queue is empty.

    5

    Report generation

    Five reports that valuation, finance and collateral teams produce every day. Choose a template and generate it from the current run.

    Sample book and prices are illustrative and not market data. Fund and counterparty names are fictional. Formulas are simplified for teaching: bond coupons, settlement lags and CSA interest are ignored.
