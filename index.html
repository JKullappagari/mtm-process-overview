Process overview

End-of-Day Mark-to-Market (MTM) Process

How a multi-asset fund turns raw vendor prices into signed-off fair values, P&L and margin calls every night, and which controls decide whether each position can flow straight through or needs a person to look at it.

High-level reference for client support, operations and valuation teams · Based on the MTM Process Explorer sample book · All figures are illustrative

1. Purpose & scope

Mark-to-market is the daily revaluation of every position at current market prices. The official end-of-day mark feeds the fund's NAV, daily P&L, risk, collateral and margin processes, and regulatory and client reporting. When a mark is wrong, every one of those outputs is wrong too, so the process is built around automated controls with human review for the exceptions.

This document describes the process for a sample APAC multi-asset fund with USD as base currency, valued at the Asia close. The book holds four asset classes:

Equities

Listed shares on SGX, HKEX and TSE. Priced at exchange close.

Bonds

Government and corporate bonds. Priced clean as % of par; valued dirty (with accrued interest).

Futures

Exchange-traded index futures. Priced at exchange settlement, margined daily.

FX forwards

OTC hedges with a bank counterparty under an ISDA CSA. Priced at forward outright.

2. Process at a glance

The nightly batch runs in six stages. Most positions go straight from left to right without anyone touching them. Positions that fail a control are routed to the exception queue, and are released only after an analyst acts on them.

3. Market data inputs

Three kinds of data arrive each night:

  • Positions. Quantity per instrument and book (shares, bond nominal, futures lots, or forward notional), plus static data such as currency, contract multiplier, coupon schedule and IFRS 13 fair value level. Each position also carries yesterday's signed-off mark, which is the baseline for move checks and P&L.
  • Vendor prices from two independent sources. The primary vendor sets the official mark. The secondary vendor is the fallback when the primary is missing, and the benchmark for independent price verification (IPV). Every price carries an as-of date.
  • FX rates, quoted as units of currency per 1 USD, again from a primary and a secondary source, alongside the prior day's rate.

4. Normalise & enrich

Before anything is valued, each vendor record goes through the same pipeline. Any instrument can be traced end to end through these four forms:

  1. Raw: the record exactly as the vendor delivered it, in the vendor's own field names and formats.
  2. Normalised: mapped to a single internal schema, whichever vendor it came from. The chosen price source (primary, secondary or analyst override) is recorded here.
  3. Enriched: static data, the applicable FX rate and the prior official mark are joined on instrument ID.
  4. Valued: the pricing model has run, producing market value, exposure and P&L, together with the formula lines behind each number.
Keeping all four forms makes every number in the final reports traceable back to the vendor file it came from. Auditors and client queries depend on that lineage.

5. Valuation

Each asset class has its own valuation model. Every result is translated into USD at today's FX rate.

Equities

MV local = quantity × close price MV USD = MV local ÷ FX(today)

Bonds

Quoted clean, valued dirty. Accrued interest uses the bond's day-count convention: ACT/ACT for government bonds and 30/360 for US corporates.

Accrued = (coupon ÷ frequency) × elapsed days ÷ days in coupon period Dirty = clean price + accrued MV local = nominal × dirty ÷ 100

Futures

Futures settle every day through the clearing house, so the carried market value resets to zero. What matters is the notional exposure and the cash variation margin.

Notional = lots × multiplier × settlement price Variation margin = lots × multiplier × (settle today − settle yesterday)

FX forwards

DF = 1 ÷ (1 + rate × days to maturity ÷ 365) MTM ccy = direction × notional × (forward outright − contract rate) × DF MTM USD = MTM ccy ÷ spot

P&L attribution

The daily P&L for each position is split into four drivers, so that analysts can see why a number moved:

6. STP controls

Six rules run on every position. A rule can pass, auto-remediate (a policy fixes the issue and the position stays straight-through), raise an exception, or require 4-eyes review. Thresholds are configurable. The defaults are shown below.

RuleControlWhat it checksDefault thresholdOutcome on failure
R1CompletenessEvery position has a price. If the primary is missing, fall back to the secondary source.Fallback enabledAuto if secondary used; Exception if no price at all
R2StalenessPrimary price is dated within N business days of the valuation date.1 business dayAuto within tolerance; Exception beyond it
R3Source variance (IPV)Primary and secondary prices agree within tolerance.0.50%Exception
R4Day-on-day moveMove versus the prior official mark stays inside the asset-class limit.Equities 8% · Bonds 2% · Futures 8% · FX fwd 1.5%Exception
R5FX availabilityEvery non-USD position has a USD rate for translation.Fallback to secondaryAuto if secondary used; Exception if none
R6Materiality (4-eyes)Daily P&L above the threshold needs a second approver before release.USD 100,000Review
Loosening a threshold raises the STP rate but lets more bad prices through. Tightening it catches more errors but adds manual work. Threshold changes should be governed by the valuation committee, not tuned night to night to get the queue to zero.

7. Statuses & STP rate

After the controls have run, each position has exactly one status:

StatusMeaningCounts as STP?
STPClean: every control passed.Yes
AUTOAuto-remediated by policy (for example a secondary-source fallback). No human action needed.Yes
REVIEWPassed every check but breached materiality. Waiting for second-person approval.No
EXCEPTIONFailed at least one control. Blocked until an analyst acts.No
RESOLVEDReleased by an analyst action (an override with a reason, or an approval).No
STP rate = (STP + AUTO) ÷ total positions

8. Exception handling

The exception queue is where valuation analysts spend their time. Each item shows the failing rule, the vendor prices, the variance and the move, so the analyst can decide quickly. There are two kinds of action:

Release with reason

For EXCEPTION items. The analyst confirms or supplies a price (or a manual FX rate) and picks a documented reason. The position becomes RESOLVED.

4-eyes approval

For REVIEW items. A second person checks the material P&L and approves it. The person who prepared the price cannot approve it.

Standard resolution reasons

  • Stale vendor price challenged; secondary accepted
  • Market move confirmed against news and exchange tape
  • Vendor error confirmed with data provider
  • Prior close carried pending vendor correction
  • Manual FX rate from WM/Reuters fix
  • Trader mark accepted after IPV review

Every override keeps the original vendor values, the price that was applied, the reason and the user. That record feeds the IPV report and the audit trail.

9. Reporting & sign-off

The batch produces a standard report pack. Each report is also available as CSV for downstream systems. The pack is marked DRAFT while any position is open, and FINAL once the queue is clear.

CodeReportContentsMain consumers
RPT-01Daily MTM valuationPosition-level fair values by book, price source and fair value levelFund accounting, NAV
RPT-02P&L attributionDaily P&L split into price, carry, FX translation and variation margin; return on NAV in bpsPortfolio managers, finance
RPT-03Price exceptions & IPVEvery control hit, the vendor variance and how it was resolvedValuation control, audit
RPT-04Margin call (CSA & ETD)OTC exposure against collateral under the CSA, and futures variation marginCollateral management, treasury
RPT-05STP control summarySTP rate, rule hit counts and the batch logOperations management

Typical batch timeline

  1. Batch started
  2. Positions extracted from the book of record
  3. Primary and secondary vendor files loaded, with FX pairs
  4. Validation complete (primary gaps identified)
  5. Valuation complete
  6. STP controls applied (released vs touched positions counted)
  7. Reports staged as DRAFT (exceptions pending) or FINAL

10. Margin calls

The official marks also drive the collateral that changes hands the next morning.

OTC bilateral (ISDA VM CSA)

The net MTM of all FX forwards with a counterparty is the exposure. It is compared with the collateral already held, and the CSA terms decide whether a call is made:

Credit support amount = max(exposure + independent amount − threshold, 0) Difference = credit support amount − collateral held If difference > MTA → delivery amount: we call (rounded UP to rounding amount) If −difference > MTA → return amount: we return (rounded DOWN) Otherwise → no call (movement below minimum transfer amount)

When exposure is negative (we owe the counterparty), expect an inbound call from them on the same logic.

Exchange-traded (futures)

Variation margin for each contract is paid or received through the clearing broker every day. The report nets the contracts into a single pay or receive figure.

12. Governance & KPIs

Segregation of duties

Front office does not set official marks. Material P&L needs a second approver, and that approver cannot be the person who prepared the price.

Independent price verification

Primary prices are checked against an independent second source every night. Persistent variances are escalated to the vendor.

Audit trail

Raw vendor values, overrides, reasons, approvers and timestamps are all retained, and every reported number traces back to its source.

Threshold governance

Tolerances and move limits are reviewed periodically by the valuation committee and change only with documented approval.

KPIs worth tracking

  • STP rate per night and its trend, broken down by asset class and vendor
  • Rule hit counts: which controls fire most, and how often an auto-fix is needed
  • Time to FINAL: from batch start until the report pack is signed off
  • Vendor quality: gaps, stale prices and IPV breaks per vendor
  • Override volume by reason code, as an early warning of a weak source or control
Simplifications in this overview: no holiday calendars (business days are weekdays only), no coupon cash flows or settlement lags, simple-interest discounting for FX forwards, USD cash collateral with no CSA interest. Production processes handle all of these.

13. Glossary

MTM
Mark-to-market: revaluing positions at current market prices.
STP
Straight-through processing: positions released without manual intervention.
IPV
Independent price verification: checking prices against an independent source.
4-eyes
A control that requires a second person to approve before release.
Clean / dirty price
A bond price without (clean) or with (dirty) accrued interest.
Variation margin (VM)
Daily cash exchanged to cover mark-to-market changes.
CSA
Credit Support Annex to an ISDA master agreement, which sets the collateral terms.
MTA
Minimum transfer amount: calls smaller than this are not made.
Threshold / IA
Unsecured exposure allowed before collateral is due / extra collateral posted regardless of MTM.
ETD
Exchange-traded derivative, such as a listed future.
IFRS 13 levels
Fair value hierarchy: Level 1 is quoted prices, Level 2 is observable inputs, Level 3 is unobservable inputs.
NAV
Net asset value of the fund.
